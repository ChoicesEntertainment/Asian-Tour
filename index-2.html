The Concept

An investor underwrites a new series of events. The Tour sells it. The investor takes the larger share of the profit.

US$30mCommitted over three seasons, drawn $10m a year
40%Of the equity in the joint venture
70%Of Series profit, ahead of the Tour
25%Of Tour profit; Growth of the Tour delivers greater profit.

The asymmetry is deliberate. The investor holds a minority of the equity but the majority of the projected income, because the investor carries the underwriting risk. The Asian Tour's priority is adding events to the schedule for its members. Income for the Asian Tour is derived from a nominal sanction fee incorporated into each event budget.

The Non-Negotiable Rule

One covenant, written into the shareholders' agreement

No tournament is confirmed until minimum thresholds are met. Applicable on day 1, with an increasing threshold in years 2 & 3.
What it protects
Commercial Viability: At a 50% margin, if successful, and a Commercial target before green light makes events viable from day 1. Guaranteed tournaments for players.
What it costs
Limits Exposure: Maximum cost of USD 10M.
Where the pressure comes from
The Test Case: New markets will want to limit contribution to see if the model is viable.

The JV guarantees 5 tournaments. It does not guarantee enhancements or investments.

Your Investment drives Growth

The schedule grows before the purse does, and never both at once

Tournaments confirmed each season

Each additional event spreads the joint venture's fixed overhead across a wider base and gives the commercial team more inventory to sell. Growth First.

Five events in 2027 is deliberately modest. Based on capital available from the investor.

From year 2 the number of events, and prize money, grow without increasing Investment. Investment is capped, growth is compounding.

By 2033 the joint venture is confirming minimum 10 tournaments a season — a schedule large enough to carry season-long sponsorship and a centrally sold media package, driving value to the whole Tour.

The purse ladder

The prize fund rises out of proven margin, not out of ambition

The purse holds at US$1 million until the tournament pool clears. Each season that clears it, the purse steps up 250,000 for the following year, up to a ceiling of US$2 million.

Prize fund per event, US$m — base case

Raising the purse does not make one event much more profitable — what it does is make the circuit worth more. A US$1.75 million event draws a stronger field and more world ranking points, which lifts host fees, title sponsorship and above all the tour-wide commercial programmes.

Two pools of profit, split differently

Because the risk in each is different

Series Revenue*

Investor takes 70%

  • Title sponsorship, host and government fees, official partners, hospitality and ticketing
  • Measured against the committed cost of each event
  • High risk, thin margin — the pool the investor's capital actually stands behind

Tour Revenue*

Split 25 / 75

  • Season-long tour partners, centrally sold media rights, official data and betting licences where lawful, merchandise
  • Grows as the purse ladder strengthens the product
  • Lower risk, higher margin — built by the Tour's team and brand

With a seventeen-event schedule the commercial pool is not a supplement to this business — it is most of it. That makes the 50/50 split the most consequential number in the term sheet.

In 2029 Asian Tour will have the option to demerge from its partnership with IMG at Asian Tour Media. This can potentially unlock very significant value as all commercial revenue will come to AT, and potential costs of delivery will drop.

The stress case the thresholds exist for: a slow commercial start

If the team contracts only half of target in 2027 and two thirds in 2028, the 25% and 40% thresholds still let the full schedule run. The venture loses US$5.4 million in 2027 and US$3.4 million in 2028, and its cash balance bottoms at US$4.6 million before recovering. The investor still earns 16.2% over the period. Had the 70% threshold applied from day one, only three events would have been confirmed in 2027 and the return would have been 16.5% — almost identical. The low early thresholds cost the investor around three tenths of a point and buy the Tour two extra tournaments in its first season.

Risks, and how each is answered

The underwriting risk is concentrated in the first two seasons

RiskAnswer
Low early thresholds expose capital to unsold eventsExposure is capped and known: about US$8.8m in 2027. The prize fund is escrowed before confirmation, and no waiver below the stated thresholds without investor consent.
The Tour's income now depends on performanceA US$0.05m sanction fee leaves the Tour participating rather than paid first. The 75% commercial share is the compensation and should be protected in the agreement.
The commercial team cannot sell at the volume assumedTranches two and three release only on confirmed-event and contracted-revenue milestones, so the sales engine is tested before the full commitment.
Concentration in one host or one sponsorWith only ten events, no host above 20% of tournament revenue and no sponsor above 25% of the commercial pool.
Host promoters default on staged paymentsDeposits or letters of credit, and no event staged while prior invoices from that host are overdue.
The venture drifts into the Tour's operating deficitsThe JV funds events only. Tour overheads and legacy subsidiaries stay outside it.
The investor cannot exitA put and call at independent fair value from 2033, with tag-along and drag-along rights.

Proposed terms, and the question to settle first

A starting position for negotiation, not a term sheet

Capital
US$30 million committed, drawn US$10 million a season from 2027, with tranche two conditional on six confirmed events for 2028 and tranche three on a tournament pool at or above break-even.
Equity and split
40% of the equity to the investor. An 8% cumulative preferred return on capital drawn, then 70% of tournament pool profit and 25% of commercial pool profit.
Green-light
Events confirmed at 25% of committed cost in 2027, 40% in 2028 and 70% from 2029, with the prize fund escrowed. No waiver below those levels without investor consent.
Prize fund
Set by the board per season: $1.00m, $1.00m, $1.25m, $1.25m, $1.50m, $1.50m, $1.75m.
Governance and exit
A board of five, three Tour and two investor, with no consent rights over sporting matters. Seven years, with a put and call at independent fair value from 2033.

US$30 million is close to three times what this plan can deploy

The peak underwriting reserve this schedule requires is US$10.9 million, and on plan assumptions about US$28 million never leaves the balance sheet. At US$15 million the same plan returns 30.6% a year rather than 18.3%. Settle the size before an investor does it for you.
